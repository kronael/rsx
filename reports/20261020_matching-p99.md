# Matching order path and checkpoint capture, p50 and p99 — 2026-10-20

**These numbers come from a noisy shared host.** The 2-vCPU VM was under
sustained CPU contention for the whole run (table below). The p50 values
repeat across runs within 3%. The p99 values do not: they move up to 2x between
runs and mostly measure the host scheduler, not the ME. Treat p99 as an upper
bound on a loaded box, not as the ME's tail. A quiet dedicated box is needed
before any of these is quoted as a floor (`BUGS.md`
MATCHING-BENCH-SHARED-HOST-VARIANCE).

## Measurement

`rsx-matching` benchmark `me_tail_bench`, two parts. Every row is one run's
samples, so p50 and p99 come from the same samples.

1. **Order path.** The production `process_order` (the bench includes
   `src/order.rs` by path; the binary does not export it): dedup, ACCEPTED
   record, `process_new_order`, `publish_events` to the WAL buffer and two
   `CastSender`s aimed at loopback sockets, order-index update, recenter
   check. The tile is the one in `tests/zero_heap_test.rs` (real book,
   `WalWriter`, dedup, index), warmed with 20,000 orders per row. Each order
   is timed on its own, so the percentiles are per-order. The peers' sockets
   are drained every 32 orders and the WAL flushed every 1,024 orders,
   between samples. Rows, at 1,000 and 10,000 resting orders per side:
   - `accept_cross`: IOC buy, one non-draining partial fill (ACCEPTED, FILL, ORDER_DONE).
   - `accept_rest`: GTC sell inside the spread (ACCEPTED, INSERTED, BBO).
   - `accept_sweep`: IOC buy taking that resting order entirely (ACCEPTED, FILL, 2 ORDER_DONE, BBO).
2. **Checkpoint capture pause.** One call of `MatchingCheckpointV1::capture`,
   the only checkpoint work on the pinned loop (every 10 s; encode and fsync
   run on the writer thread). The snapshot is dropped outside the sample, as
   the writer thread drops it. `capture/<n>` is a book of `n` live orders, 4 per
   price level, both sides. `capture/1000000_slots_10pct_live` is the
   1,000,000-order book with nine orders in ten cancelled: live orders
   100,000, slab high-water mark 1,000,000.

## Method

- Source benchmark: `rsx-matching/benches/me_tail_bench.rs`; command
  `cargo bench -p rsx-matching --bench me_tail_bench`
- Source commit: `1a41f620` (the bench); runs 2 and 3 ran at HEAD `08bd3306`.
  Run 1 used the same measurement code before it was committed, without the
  context-switch column.
- Toolchain: `rustc 1.101.0-nightly (07e526db9 2026-10-19)`, `bench` profile
  (release, no LTO, 16 codegen units)
- Host: AMD Ryzen 9 5950X, virtualized (hypervisor flag set), 2 vCPUs, 3.9 GiB
- Percentiles: nearest rank over all samples. Order rows: 100,000 samples, so
  p99 is the 1,000th-largest. Capture rows: 500 to 3,000 samples, so p99 is
  the 5th-largest at 1,000,000 orders and the 10th at 100,000.
- Timer floor (back-to-back `Instant::now()`): p50 20 ns, p99 31 ns, under 0.2%
  of the cheapest order sample.
- Gate: p50 and p99 of each row go to `target/tail/rsx-matching.json`;
  `scripts/bench-gate.sh` compares them with `bench-baseline.json`. The
  baseline holds the largest of the three runs for each entry.

## Conclusion

- **Order path p50 is stable and depth-independent in this range.** 20.9–21.3 µs
  for `accept_cross` and `accept_rest`, 34.9–35.3 µs for `accept_sweep`, the
  same at 1,000 and 10,000 resting orders per side, within 3% across runs.
- **The order path costs 21 µs for 3 records and 35 µs for 5, about 7 µs per
  record, against 266–295 ns for the Criterion `me_accept_path/full`**
  (`reports/20260709_matching-benches.md`). That bench omits the cast sends and
  the metrics. Each record is one WAL append and two `sendto` calls, so the cost
  tracks the record count, not the order type. This is an inference from the
  scaling; the path was not profiled.
- **Order path p99 is 95–130 µs for cross and rest, 300–810 µs for sweep, on
  this host.** The p99.9 (13–20 ms) and max (34–56 ms) are scheduler delays: the
  bench thread was preempted 2,000–6,250 times per row. The mean is 3–6x the
  p50 for the same reason. With the top percent holding multi-millisecond
  samples, p99 sits at the edge of the preempted population, which is why it
  moves between runs and why the longer `accept_sweep` op shows the highest p99.
  That reading is an inference from the context-switch counts; the host's
  contribution to p99 itself was not separated from the ME's.
- **The capture pause grows steeply with the book.** p50 0.15–0.22 ms at 1,000
  orders, 0.24–0.30 ms at 10,000, 17–19 ms at 100,000, 209–262 ms at
  1,000,000. The pause holds the pinned loop: orders arriving during it wait
  in the UDP receive buffer. Whether a pause of that length trips a Risk or ME
  timeout is not measured.
- **The pause follows the slab high-water mark, not the live order count.**
  With 100,000 live orders on a 1,000,000-slot slab it is 148–157 ms p50,
  against 17–19 ms for a fresh 100,000-order book. `capture` sweeps every slab
  slot up to the high-water mark and walks the free list (read from
  `rsx-book/src/snapshot.rs`; the split of the time between the two was not
  profiled).
- The `capture/1000` and `capture/10000` p99 (11–21 ms) is 50–100x their p50
  and is host preemption, not capture work.

## Caveats

- **Noisy shared host**, stated at the top. `/proc/pressure/cpu` `some` stayed
  between 86% and 95% in all three runs. `BENCH_CORE` is 2 and the VM has 2
  vCPUs, so the harness fell back to core 0, shared with the other load.
- Single thread, in-process, closed-loop: one order at a time, no queueing.
  This is service time, not latency under load.
- The cast peers are loopback sockets drained by the bench thread between
  samples, not separate processes. Loopback delivers inside the sender's
  `sendto`, so the order cost includes the receive-queue enqueue that a NIC send
  would not.
- WAL flush (fsync every 1,024 orders, about 10 ms of orders) is outside the
  samples. A flush on the pinned loop delays the next order; that delay is not
  in these rows.
- Synthetic books: one order per tick (order path), 4 orders per price level
  (capture). The real resting size and level shape of a live symbol are
  unknown here. Dedup and index maps grow during a row by design (about
  340,000 orders per tile), so a map resize can appear in the tail.
- Capture: 500 samples at 1,000,000 orders make p99 the 5th-largest value.
- The checked-in gate baseline is the largest of three noisy runs. The gate fails
  only on a regression beyond that spread, and it can still flake on this
  host. It is meaningful on a quiet box only.
